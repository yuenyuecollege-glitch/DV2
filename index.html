Who still shows up?

Formal volunteering through organisations has slipped since before COVID — but Australians still give hundreds of millions of hours, and regional communities lean on volunteers more than the big cities.

22.6% of adults did unpaid voluntary work through an organisation in 2025
618M formal volunteer hours in the prior 12 months — back above the 2020 dip
34% vs 21% remote Australia vs major cities formal volunteering rate

01 — Locate

Where people still volunteer

Participation is uneven. Census 2021 maps show regional SA4s with higher rates, while absolute headcounts pile into New South Wales and Victoria. The GSS remoteness gap is stark: remote Australia volunteers formally at 34%, major cities at 21%.

ABS General Social Survey 2025. One gap, two places: major cities at 20.9% versus remote Australia at 34.0%.

Regional and remote communities still lean harder on formal volunteering than the capitals — even after a national decline.

02 — Identify

Who steps forward

Mid-life adults carry formal volunteering. People aged 45–54 sit at 28%; those aged 25–34 are at 13%. Since 2019, both formal and informal rates have softened — formal more sharply.

C5 · Rate by age

Lollipop of GSS 2025 formal rates. Highlighted ends: peak mid-life vs the softest working-age band.

C6 · 2019 to 2025 slope

Formal volunteering fell from 29.5% to 22.6%. Informal help also eased, but stayed higher.

03 — Compare

What they do

Among formal volunteers, sport and recreation organisations lead (33%), then faith groups (23%) and community or cultural groups (18%). Broader “volunteering” also includes informal help — Snapshot 2023 estimates how those roles overlap.

Treemap sized by GSS 2025 shares. People can name more than one organisation type, so shares need not sum to 100.

C8 · Formal, informal, both — or neither

Snapshot of Volunteering in Australia 2023. Flows are population shares. “Neither” is implied from formal-only + informal-only + both (about 50%); Snapshot’s headline “any volunteering” figure (64%) uses a broader framing — treat this diagram as a structure view, not an ABS official split.

04 — Summarise

Hours and contribution

The participation rate stayed soft, but total formal hours recovered: 596 million in 2019, 490 million in 2020, 618 million in 2025. Older volunteers are more likely to give 100 hours or more. Charities report about 3.77 million volunteers in their own books.

Unit charts. Left: GSS 2025 formal volunteering (~23%). Right: Census 2021 (~14%), collected during COVID-affected conditions with a different question timing. Same idea, different instruments — do not splice them into one trend.

ACNC’s 2023 charity filings count about 3.77 million volunteers on organisation books — overlapping with, but not identical to, the ABS person surveys.

05 — Explain

Why people don’t — and what organisations face

Among non-volunteers, “no time” (47%) and “not interested or no need” (36%) dominate. Volunteers themselves most often cite helping others and personal satisfaction. Time pressure and motivation sit on opposite sides of the same ledger.

C12 · Reasons for and against

Diverging bars from GSS 2025. Right: reasons among formal volunteers. Left: reasons among people who did not volunteer formally. People could choose more than one reason.

For volunteer-involving organisations, the message is plain: schedule flexibility fights “no time,” and a clear ask fights “never been asked” — while sport, faith and community groups remain the largest formal homes for help.
